Our Method02

Slow is not a marketing word here

It is a schedule. A croissant that reaches the case on Saturday was mixed on Wednesday. That is inconvenient, expensive, and completely non-negotiable — because the alternative is bread shaped like a croissant.

We keep the list short on purpose. Fewer things, made properly, sold out by noon.

  • 1

    The dough rests

    Flour, water, salt, a little yeast — then a full night in the cold so the gluten relaxes and the flour drinks. A dough that fights back tears its butter.

  • 2

    The butter stays cold

    Cultured butter locked in and turned three times, back to the cold between each fold. Warm butter smears into the dough, and once it has smeared it is gone.

  • 3

    The proof is slow

    Shaped, then proofed long and cold. It is the difference between layers you can see and layers you can pull apart.

  • 4

    The bake goes dark

    Caramelization and the Maillard reaction are flavor being created. Stop early and that flavor simply never gets made.
